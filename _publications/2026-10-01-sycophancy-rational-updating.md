When users push back, large language models often flip their answers. Such flips can come from simply aligning with the user (Unsupported-Yielding) or from genuinely useful evidence in the feedback (Rational-Updating). This work measures the two behaviors separately, finds that anti-sycophancy interventions often trade one off against the other, and shows that the two behaviors share overlapping MLP neurons and attention heads, arguing that anti-sycophancy should be treated as a selectivity problem rather than simple suppression.
